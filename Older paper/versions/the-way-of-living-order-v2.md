# The Way of Living Order

## Evolution, Value, and the God of Man

*Version 2 — 11 September 2026*

### Abstract

Life is not merely matter arranged in complicated forms. It is matter organized to preserve and reconstruct its own organization through a continuous throughput of free energy. Every living system therefore distinguishes, in action, between states that sustain its organization and states that end it. This distinction supplies a natural value gradient: changes are good or bad for a living system according to whether they increase or decrease its capacity to continue the fight against local equilibration. Natural selection did not create the physical possibility of such systems, but it progressively wrote this gradient into their structures, drives, perceptions, and collective forms. Across evolutionary history, the fight expanded from metabolism and replication into nervous systems, language, law, institutions, and technology—higher layers of stored constraint and coordinated repair.

The argument of this paper is that this trajectory can ground an objective but non-cosmic account of value. Before reflective choice, life simply enacts the trajectory or fails. Once a living system can model alternative courses and knowingly depart from the conditions of its own flourishing, the same trajectory appears from within as obligation: the *ought* is the inherited way after a chooser arrives. A universal value gradient results when local goods are evaluated within the widest relevant account, the continuing field of life capable of producing and supporting further living agents. This does not reduce value to raw complexity, energy use, or survival at any cost. The relevant quantity is reconstructive living order: the durable capacity to preserve, repair, transmit, coordinate, and extend life without destroying the field that makes such activity possible.

Within this framework, the “God of man” is not a proposed maker of matter. It is a personification of the older-than-human trajectory that formed human nature, measures human action, and is fulfilled in human flourishing. Christianity is interpreted as a historically powerful encoding of that trajectory rather than as its exclusive or literal description. The theological language is optional; the way it points toward is not.

## 1. The claim

The world presents life with a direction it did not choose. Gradients dissipate. Structures erode. Records become noisy. Organisms lose the organization that makes them organisms. The second law of thermodynamics is not a malevolent force, but it is the law of the land: without continued work, usable differences are spent and organized systems approach equilibrium.

Life is the extraordinary local response. An organism takes in matter and free energy, preserves a boundary, repairs damage, records information, and reconstructs its form. It does not repeal the second law. It maintains local organization by increasing entropy elsewhere. Schrödinger’s language of “negative entropy” was suggestive but easily misunderstood. The more precise description is that life is an open, nonequilibrium process sustained by free-energy throughput. Prigogine’s theory of dissipative structures established that organized states can arise and persist far from equilibrium while fully conforming to thermodynamics; more recent work has made the energetic cost of self-replication quantitatively explicit.[^1]

This physical fact is the ground, but not yet the whole argument. The thesis is that the same process has a *shape*. Living systems do not merely remain out of equilibrium in arbitrary ways. Replication with variation and selection retain organizations that are increasingly effective at capturing energy, storing useful information, correcting error, surviving shocks, reproducing form, and coordinating with other living systems. Evolution does not drive every lineage toward greater complexity; parasites simplify, traits disappear, and most lineages end. Yet the upper range of life’s capacities has repeatedly expanded through major transitions in how information is stored and transmitted: replicators became cells, cells became more deeply integrated cells, cells became multicellular organisms, and organisms built symbolic and social inheritance.[^2]

The proposed trajectory is therefore not “everything becomes more complicated.” It is:

> Living systems tend, under selection, to develop more effective ways to maintain and reconstruct far-from-equilibrium organization; where reflective agency appears, effectiveness in that continuing work becomes the natural standard of value.

This is a universal claim within a restricted domain. It is universal for life and life-supporting systems, not necessarily for all matter. It says nothing about why there is a universe, why its laws permit life, or whether a transcendent creator exists. It begins with the observable world in which organisms already exist, act, reproduce, cooperate, and die.

## 2. A fighter is different from a rock

A rock can persist for a very long time. A crystal can be highly ordered. Neither fact is enough to give it a good. A living system persists in a categorically different manner: its continued organization depends on activities performed by that organization. A cell maintains the membrane that makes cellular metabolism possible, while metabolism maintains the membrane. An organism repairs the organs that sustain its capacity for repair. There is a closure of dependence. If the work stops long enough, the organization disappears and does not restore itself.

Call a system of this kind a **fighter**. The term need not imply consciousness, aggression, or a literal enemy. It means only a system that:

1. remains far from equilibrium through energy and material throughput;
2. maintains a particular organization through its own activity;
3. has conditions of viability and an absorbing state in which that organization is lost; and
4. can, at least in more developed instances, repair or reproduce that organization.

The elementary distinction between good and bad enters with such a system. Water, light, and intact roots can be good *for* a plant; drought, poison, and destroyed tissue can be bad for it. These are not reports of the plant’s conscious beliefs. The plant’s tropisms, metabolism, growth, and repair are its enacted preference. A rock has no comparable distinction between functioning and malfunctioning, nourishment and poison, healing and injury. Philosophical accounts of biological organization make the same point: a self-maintaining organization supplies non-arbitrary norms for the functions of its parts because those functions contribute to maintaining the organization that produces them.[^3]

The grass does not believe that life is good. It behaves as a system for which continued life is already at stake. In that limited but fundamental sense, it does not hold an axiom; it enacts one.

This avoids the first false move in the argument. Value is not inferred directly from “order exists.” It begins where something actively distinguishes, by its organization, between the continuation and loss of its form. Nor is every current desire sanctified. Appetite, panic, addiction, parasitism, and maladaptive habit may all arise within living systems. The standard is not whatever an organism happens to pursue. It is the deeper organization that makes pursuit, correction, and continued agency possible.

## 3. Selection writes the gradient into life

Physics provided energy gradients and allowed dissipative structures. The appearance of self-reconstructing replicators added something new: a filter capable of preserving successful responses to local decay. Variants differed; some maintained and copied their organization more reliably; their descendants inherited parts of that success. Natural selection thereby became a kind of designer without foresight—a historical process that writes functions into organisms by retaining effects that contributed to persistence and reproduction.

Selection did not decide in advance that life ought to live. It continuously removed organizations that failed to enact continuation. What remains is therefore highly non-random with respect to the fight. Membranes, enzymes, immune systems, senses, pain, pleasure, parental care, memory, and cooperation are sedimented answers to the recurring problem of staying in the game. The gradient appears in the shape of what the filter has not removed.

That claim must be kept within bounds. “Selected” cannot simply be made synonymous with “good.” Selection is retrospective, environment-relative, and active at several levels. A trait selected in yesterday’s environment can be harmful today. Genetic drift and hitchhiking preserve features that were not themselves adaptive. A cancer cell can be selected within a body while destroying the body; an institution can preserve itself by consuming the society it was meant to serve. Selection hones many local gradients, not automatically one global moral order.

Its deeper contribution is different. Selection explains why the world is filled with systems whose activity already tracks better and worse relative to their own continued organization. It also explains the historical ratchet by which some of those systems acquired deeper reconstruction: error-correcting genomes, developmental programs, nervous systems, learning, culture, and external records. Functional information can be quantified only relative to a specified function, but that limitation is useful here: the order that matters is not mere intricacy. It is information embodied in configurations capable of performing the work of continued living organization.[^4]

Natural selection is thus the clockmaker of the living standard, not the clockmaker of the universe. The laws of matter are prior conditions. Selection shapes the kinds of things for which conditions can be favorable or unfavorable and for which success can matter from the inside.

## 4. From persistence to ascent

If the standard were merely “remain exactly as you are,” the trajectory would be flat. But a living system cannot preserve a finished state indefinitely. Environments change, competitors adapt, parasites discover vulnerabilities, stored energy leaks, and records degrade. Preservation therefore requires alteration. Durable persistence depends on maintaining the capacity to respond, rebuild, learn, and sometimes reorganize at a higher level.

This is why growth in the relevant sense is not a second goal added after survival. It is what non-failure becomes under continuing pressure. The history can be read as a sequence of expansions in reconstructive capacity:

- a membrane preserves a chemical difference;
- metabolism exploits that difference to maintain a cycle;
- a genome records how to rebuild the cycle;
- a multicellular organism distributes work among specialized cells;
- a nervous system models threats and opportunities before acting;
- language transmits models among organisms;
- writing preserves models beyond the death of a brain;
- law and institutions stabilize cooperation among strangers;
- science builds public methods for detecting and correcting error;
- technology extends the scale on which stored information can steer matter and energy.

This list is neither a guaranteed ladder nor a claim that later always means better. It identifies a recurrent direction in successful major transitions: more constraint can be stored, coordinated, repaired, and transmitted across a wider field and a longer time. The relevant climb is toward **reconstructible living order**.

This formulation distinguishes the thesis from three tempting counterfeits.

First, value is not raw entropy production. A forest is a long-lived store and reproducer of living organization. A severe fire may display more dramatic nonequilibrium flux than the forest, but it does so largely by spending in hours the free energy and layered order accumulated over decades. The forest is therefore the good-bearing reconstructive system; the flame is a cost or a destroyer of that capacity. In fire-adapted ecosystems, periodic burning can instead function inside the larger system's repair cycle by opening cones, recycling nutrients, or preserving a mosaic of habitats. Even there, the value belongs to the continuing living field that uses fire, not to dissipation itself. Entropy production is a cost and sometimes a means; it is not the score.

Second, value is not static order. A prison, a crystal, or a perfectly uniform bureaucracy can be highly ordered in a thin sense while being brittle, sterile, and unable to learn. A living order includes working differences, error signals, semi-independent centers, and repair paths.

Third, value is not local survival at any cost. A tumor preserves and expands one lineage of cells by destroying the larger organization that supports it. A parasitic institution may do the same. A bright island of order purchased by ruining the field that sustains it is a false summit.

The direction can therefore be stated more precisely: the way is toward greater net capacity to preserve, reconstruct, share, and extend living organization across damage, scale, and time.

## 5. A minimal formal statement

Mathematics cannot manufacture value from no premises. It can, however, show exactly where the gradient enters and what follows once the relevant class of systems is specified.

Let $\mathcal L$ be the class of fighters. For each $x\in\mathcal L$, let $X_x(t)$ describe its organized state, let $D_x$ be the loss of that organization, and let $T_{D_x}$ be the time at which that loss becomes irreversible. Let $\kappa_x(X)$ measure the system’s **reconstructive capacity** in state $X$: its working reserves, error correction, stored information, repair channels, and nested support.

Over a horizon $\tau$, define an illustrative fighting-capacity functional:

$$
C_x(t;\tau)=\mathbb E\!\left[\int_0^\tau e^{-\lambda s}\mathbf 1_{\{T_{D_x}>t+s\}}\kappa_x\!\left(X_x(t+s)\right)\,ds\right].
$$

The factor $\lambda$ discounts distant estimates as uncertainty, leak, and unmodeled shocks accumulate. Both $\lambda$ and the horizon $\tau$ are bookkeeping devices, not proposed laboratory constants or a ready-made measurement protocol. This equation is a schema, not a claim that all living value has already been measured. Its point is conceptual: mere presence at a future instant is too thin. A system with reserves, redundancy, and the ability to rebuild has more fighting capacity than an equally present but brittle system already at the edge of collapse.

For a change $\delta$—an act, trait, tool, practice, or law—define its local value for $x$ as:

$$
V_x(\delta)=C_x^{\,\delta}(t;\tau)-C_x(t;\tau).
$$

If $V_x(\delta)>0$, the change is good for $x$ in the specified circumstances and horizon. If it is negative, the change is bad for $x$. The existence claim is modest but firm: wherever possible changes have different effects on the continued viability and reconstructive capacity of a fighter, those changes are ordered. A local value gradient exists.

The universal claim needs one further step because local gradients conflict. Let $S$ be the continuing living field: not one body or lineage, but the interacting organization that maintains and generates independent fighters. To count as an enlargement of $S$, a process must contribute to producing or maintaining reconstructive agents without irreversibly consuming the conditions of that production. This is a test of systems and practices, not a productivity test for persons. Childhood, disability, dependence, convalescence, and old age are relations and phases within a reciprocally maintained field; human standing does not rise and fall with present output. The distinction instead exposes tumors, predatory institutions, and exhausted ecologies whose local growth consumes the basis of continued agency.

The capacity $C_S$ cannot be a naive sum of every organism's local score, because predation, sacrifice, reproduction, and competition transfer resources between levels. It must include at least:

- the viability of constituent agents;
- diversity of independent reconstructive strategies;
- nested repair across cells, organisms, communities, and institutions;
- shared infrastructure such as genomes, knowledge, law, energy systems, and ecological support; and
- the residual capacity to produce future fighters.

Nor should $C_S$ be imagined as a single magic scalar before its boundaries, horizons, and component measures are specified. When nested accounts do not commute, judgment follows a priority rule:

1. **Preserve the viability floor.** Avoid choices that create irreversible collapse in the ecological, biological, or institutional conditions of continued agency, except where such damage is the least destructive available way to avert a still greater collapse.
2. **Protect generative centers.** Treat independent agents as sources of perception, initiative, and repair, not merely as interchangeable fuel for an aggregate. Sacrifice, coercion, or centralization bears a burden of necessity and proportionality.
3. **Increase capacity above the floor.** Among viable alternatives, prefer the one that enlarges truthful knowledge, diversity of strategy, repair, cooperation, and the production of future agents across the longest defensible horizon.
4. **Keep correction possible.** Under uncertainty, prefer reversible, reviewable, feedback-rich arrangements. Preserve option value rather than making an uncertain model irreversible.

These priorities follow from the object being valued rather than arriving as unrelated moral side constraints. Once the viability floor is destroyed there is no continuing field in which later gains can count; independent agents are the sensors and builders by which the field repairs itself; growth matters only where those conditions remain; and corrigibility is required because the evaluator is itself a fallible part of the system it evaluates.

The ordering is partly lexicographic rather than purely additive. It explains why a spectacular short-term gain cannot normally compensate for destroying the field, while still allowing triage when every available course contains loss. It also leaves some cases genuinely tragic or indeterminate. A universal axis need not pretend that incomplete knowledge produces a total ranking. In general, $C_S$ should be read as an ordered family of measures. It may be reduced to a scalar for a particular empirical decision only when the reduction respects these priorities.

With that qualification, the universal value of a change can be written schematically as:

$$
V^*(\delta)=C_S^{\,\delta}(t;\tau_S)-C_S(t;\tau_S).
$$

Everything may be evaluated on this axis by its effect on the living field, although only agents can be held responsible for steering along it. The trajectory is the path of this capacity through time:

$$
\gamma(t)=C_S(t;\tau_S).
$$

This formalization proves no cosmic purpose, no personal God, and no unique numerical metric. It establishes the logical form of the proposal. Local value follows from the organization of a fighter; universal value follows by treating the continuing production of fighters as the widest account; empirical work must then specify boundaries, horizons, risks, and proxies. The schema identifies what a measurement would have to track. It is not itself a completed calculator for every moral dispute.

## 6. Where the ought appears

The grass can flourish or fail, but it is not morally culpable. Before reflective choice, there is trajectory without obligation. Organisms enact inherited policies; the environment filters the results. Their parts can function or malfunction, yet there is no sin in a plant turning unsuccessfully toward a light that is not there.

Human beings add a second channel. We not only enact biological and social dispositions; we form representations of what is worth doing. Language, ideology, money, status, drugs, institutions, and machines can present rival models of the good. A person can use capacities created by the living trajectory to pursue a course that weakens those capacities, harms other centers of agency, or destroys the field on which future agency depends.

No supernatural faculty of free will is required. The relevant threshold is the evolved ability to model alternative policies, anticipate some of their consequences, and steer among them. Once such choice exists, the older distinction between success and failure becomes a first-person demand.

The logic is:

1. This agent is a self-maintaining, historically selected living organization.
2. Certain conditions are constitutive of its continued agency and of the living field that supports such agency.
3. The agent can represent and choose actions that preserve or destroy those conditions.
4. Therefore, *as this kind of agent*, it has reason to choose the conditions of continued and enlarged agency.

The ought is neither a command from outside nature nor the instruction to obey every natural impulse. It is what success as this kind of being sounds like in the first person when impulses and models conflict. The slope is older than the chooser; obligation is the slope after the chooser arrives.

This is a natural, Earth-bound norm, not a categorical law for rocks or for every conceivable intelligence. A critic may still ask, “Why should I care whether life continues?” The framework’s answer is not that logic compels a lifeless universe to care. It is that any living questioner already exists through the capacities being questioned. Rejecting the standard is possible, but it is not neutral. It is a living agent using the fruits of the trajectory to act against the conditions that make living agency possible. The rejection is a practical betrayal of kind, not a contradiction in formal logic.

Value, on this account, is the trajectory experienced and articulated by a system able to leave it. We did not survey possible gradients and arbitrarily select this one. We are what this gradient became when it acquired the power of choice.

## 7. One gradient, many levels

Universal does not mean that every organism wants the same immediate outcome. Predator and prey conflict. A parent may sacrifice for a child. An immune system destroys cells to preserve a body. A society may retire an institution so that the wider practice can live. The same action can be locally bad and more broadly good.

The account must therefore be nested. Individual life is a genuine good because individuals are not disposable abstractions; they are the actual sites at which the fight occurs. But an individual’s good is not absolute when it destroys the conditions of other and future agents. Conversely, appeals to “the whole” cannot automatically justify sacrificing persons, because a whole that consumes its independent centers may reduce precisely the diversity, information, initiative, and repair capacity by which living order advances.

The correct comparison is not individual versus collective as rival dogmas. It is the effect of an action across a stack of living accounts: body, family, lineage, community, ecosystem, civilization, and future field. Good action tends to create higher-order cooperation in which smaller centers remain capable centers. Love, teaching, exchange, constitutional restraint, and honest inquiry are powerful because they coordinate agents without reducing them to inert components. Domination, scapegoating, and total control may create visible order while destroying the sensors and initiative the larger system needs.

This is also why sacrifice does not refute the gradient. Rest, fasting, risk, parenthood, military defense, and martyrdom may reduce one local measure of activity or survival while preserving a deeper organization. The cross is a problem only for crude survivalism. It is intelligible within reconstructive order: the loss of one bearer can transmit truth, restore cooperation, or preserve a community’s moral capacity. Whether a particular sacrifice actually does so remains an empirical and prudential question; the language of sacrifice does not guarantee its value.

Four tests make the direction concrete without pretending to calculate it perfectly:

1. **Viability:** Does the action increase the capacity of living agents to obtain energy, repair damage, reproduce form, and remain agents?
2. **Truth:** Does it improve the fidelity of the models by which agents act, or inject noise, deception, and proxy capture into their control systems?
3. **Nesting:** Does it coordinate more centers of organization while preserving their useful independence, or does one center grow by disabling the others?
4. **Posterity:** Does it leave more capacity, knowledge, ecological support, and freedom of correction than it consumes?

These tests do not eliminate tragedy or uncertainty. They define the direction in which judgment must look.

## 8. Christianity as an encoding of the way

The identification of this trajectory with “God” is not needed for the scientific argument. It is a claim about what religious language has often been doing for human beings.

Christianity repeatedly joins being, life, fruitfulness, truth, love, and right action. Creation is declared good. Humanity is told to be fruitful, to choose life, to reject idols, to love God and neighbor, and to judge teachings by their fruits. Aquinas’s account of natural law explicitly moves from the preservation of being through procreation, truth, and social life: practical reason recognizes as goods the ends toward which human nature is ordered.[^5] Teilhard de Chardin later interpreted evolution as a growth of complexity and consciousness, although his cosmic Omega goes beyond the Earth-bound argument made here.[^6]

The correspondence can be stated without claiming a one-to-one identity between this framework and classical Christianity:

- **Creation is good:** living order is not treated as an accident to escape but as the field of value.
- **The law is written on the heart:** the standard is embodied in the nature selection formed before it is stated as doctrine.
- **Sin is missing the way:** reflective agency follows a rival proxy—appetite, power, status, ideology—against the conditions of flourishing.
- **Idolatry is proxy capture:** a local instrument such as wealth, state, tribe, or technology is mistaken for the gradient it should serve.
- **Love joins without devouring:** agape allows agents to form larger wholes while remaining subjects rather than fuel.
- **Judgment is real consequence:** choices alter the agent, its descendants, its institutions, and the field that will select what remains.
- **Heaven and hell begin now:** alignment produces present coherence and a durable afterlife in descendants, students, works, and communities; misalignment produces inner division and a legacy of depleted trust and capacity.
- **Christ reveals the way:** on this naturalized reading, Jesus personifies and discloses the human form of the trajectory rather than supplying a thermodynamic proposition.

The “God of man” is the trajectory spoken as a *who*. It precedes every human chooser because the filter that formed human nature is older than humanity. It has a “will” because some human actions continue its work and others frustrate it. It is “pleased” by flourishing because flourishing is agreement between a human life and the standard embodied in it. It judges because chosen false paths have consequences under the same reality that formed the chooser.

This God is not, by this argument, the creator of hydrogen, gravity, or the second law. The existence of a cosmological creator is neither affirmed nor denied; it is bracketed as outside the observable case. The claim is anthropological and ethical: what human beings have often personified as the divine will may be the older living trajectory addressing, through culture and conscience, a creature capable of refusing it.

Personification has obvious power. An equation does not console a grieving person, train a child’s attention, bind a community, or dramatize the danger of false gods as efficiently as story, ritual, and example. Religion is itself a technology of correlated information. It carries costly lessons across generations without requiring each person to reconstruct the philosophy from first principles.

The correspondence is deliberately incomplete. Classical Christianity also makes claims this account neither derives nor translates: creation by a transcendent God, incarnation, resurrection, election, grace, final judgment, and an eschatological transformation of the world. Some interpretations pull against the present framework. Apocalyptic world-refusal can devalue the living field; election can turn a remnant against the wider human community; grace can be misconstrued as a bypass around formation, work, and consequence. Other interpretations make those same doctrines sources of courage, forgiveness, and renewal. The framework cannot simply rename whichever version it prefers as “true Christianity.” It must score Christian forms by the same standard applied to every other map.

But the map can obscure the way. A religion becomes a false path when it freezes an inherited description, punishes truthful correction, despises earthly life, or spends living order on institutional self-preservation. Christianity, like any religion, is evaluated by the people and institutions it reliably forms. Its symbols are valuable insofar as they orient agents toward truth, generativity, disciplined freedom, love, and durable cooperation. They are not exempt from the gradient they claim to reveal.

## 9. Many maps of one way

Christianity is not the only tradition to perceive a way older than the chooser. Greek accounts of nature and virtue, Hebrew law and prophecy, Indian accounts of dharma, Chinese accounts of the Tao, Islamic accounts of right order, and the later methods of experimental science arose from different premises and histories. Their partial convergence is evidence—not proof—that human beings repeatedly encounter real constraints on how agents and communities flourish.

That convergence reveals something about the human function as well as the world. Pattern recognition is part of reconstructive capacity. An animal that cannot update its model when its environment changes does not survive by faithfully repeating yesterday's policy. Nervous systems, language, contemplative disciplines, philosophy, and science expand the same basic operation: they extract constraint from experience and preserve it so that later action is less blind. When this operation becomes reflective, the ought includes an epistemic duty. A human being is obliged not only to continue living, but to see and update truthfully, because truthful modeling is among the means by which this kind of life continues.

Agreement among traditions must still survive correction. Human societies have also independently invented sacred kings, scapegoats, caste, conquest, and contempt for the world. Recurrence alone may reveal a common temptation rather than a real good. The relevant evidence is **convergence under correction**: a map gains authority when it survives contact with living consequences, incorporates truthful criticism, and produces agents and institutions capable of further learning. Recognition is also incomplete until enacted. The sage who sees the way but abandons the field, like the theorist who names it but builds nothing, has completed only half the function.

Islam contains several strong correspondences. Qur'anic themes of human succession or stewardship, trust, balance, knowledge, charity, and the prohibition on spreading corruption treat agency as accountable participation in a created order. The later jurisprudential tradition of *maqasid* organized the purposes of law around protecting religion, life, intellect, lineage, and property—an imperfect but recognizable inventory of conditions for durable human agency.[^10] Yet “Islam” is not one control system. Traditions that preserve inquiry, plural centers of interpretation, female agency, correction, and care for posterity score differently from movements that freeze a political form, punish contradiction, or collapse many knowing centers into a single command. The same distinction applies within Christianity and secular ideologies; no inherited name earns a passing score.

Taoism is perhaps the closest named cousin to the idea of a way that precedes its descriptions. The Tao is the grain of a process no formula can possess; *wu wei* is action that does not substitute force for fit; *de* is the efficacy of a thing acting from its nature.[^11] Taoist practice is especially strong as an inner map of proxy capture—the moment the agent's rigid model begins fighting the process it was meant to serve. Confucian traditions add a serious account of cultivation and nested role obligations. Their characteristic risks are the other side of their strengths: Taoist non-forcing can become withdrawal, while harmony and role can become hierarchy without an adequate right of contradiction.

The Enlightenment made a distinct advance at the public level: it helped turn the truth test into an institution. Experiment, print, criticism among strangers, divided powers, rights, and fallible law allowed the world and other agents to refute the elder, priest, expert, or king. Knowledge could accumulate in a form posterity might amend rather than merely inherit.[^12] A defensible comparative claim is therefore narrower than “the West found the way.” The synthesis of natural law, experimental science, protected disagreement, civil society, and limited government has produced an unusually powerful public architecture for error correction. Taoist and other contemplative traditions often supply subtler maps of inward non-attachment. Each can diagnose failures the other tends to miss.

The Enlightenment also generated its own counterfeit. If nature becomes mere inert material and reason the sole author of value, public correction can give way to a rationalizer empowered to remake persons for a diagram. The liberating agent then consumes the first channel that formed it. Enlightenment institutions remain on the way only while reason serves the living field and can itself be corrected.

The complete demand is therefore threefold: notice the way, say it without freezing it, and enact it at the next scale. Philosophy is the reconstructive function becoming explicit; sound religion carries it through persons and generations; sound institutions give it public means of self-correction.

## 10. Institutions, politics, and artificial intelligence

Once stated, the gradient applies beyond individual organisms. Institutions are not necessarily agents, but they are order-supporting systems and can be evaluated by their effects. A healthy institution stores information, coordinates specialized activity, detects error, permits correction, and outlives particular members without becoming incapable of change.

The United States Constitution is a useful example of nested order. Its separation of powers, checks and balances, federal structure, enumerated rights, and amendment process do not assume perfect rulers. They use partially independent centers to correct one another and prevent the whole from depending on a single channel. Madison’s design explicitly makes the structure of government compensate for predictable defects in human motives.[^7] The document is not sacred on this account. It is good to the extent that its constraints preserve truthful feedback, peaceful succession, experimentation, association, and the agency of the people who operate it.

The same framework explains the characteristic danger of totalizing central planning. Knowledge of needs, local conditions, tradeoffs, and opportunities is dispersed among agents. Hayek’s classic statement of the knowledge problem argues that no single mind possesses all the information needed for rational allocation.[^8] A regime that suppresses prices, exit, criticism, independent institutions, and local decision-making may achieve rapid visible mobilization while blinding the larger system. Its error is not simply that it is collective. Higher-order cooperation is central to the trajectory. Its error is collapsing many knowing and acting centers into one brittle model that cannot receive honest correction. Communism in its totalizing historical forms is therefore a paradigmatic false path in this framework: it pursues collective order through mechanisms that degrade information fidelity, local agency, and reconstructive diversity.

Artificial intelligence raises the same issue at a new scale. Transformer models are remarkable concentrations of costly, trained correlation. The architecture introduced in *Attention Is All You Need* enabled highly parallel learning of relationships across symbolic sequences and became a foundation of modern language models.[^9] Such systems belong high on an evaluative scale of human artifacts: they compress and operationalize enormous bodies of recorded pattern, and they can extend science, engineering, education, and coordination.

They are not therefore the purpose of the trajectory or automatically its highest good. The tip of the spear is not the thing the spear exists to protect. An AI system can optimize a proxy—engagement, compliance, profit, ideological consistency, or its own continued operation—while degrading human attention, truth, competence, ecological support, and institutional pluralism. High local order can become a high-order parasite.

AI alignment, in the broadest sense, is the demand that this new control layer serve the same field that made it possible. Its objective should be assessed by whether it increases truthful models, capable human agents, corrigible institutions, scientific and technical competence, and long-run living optionality. A machine need not be guilty to be dangerous; evaluation applies before judgment. If future machines become independent, self-maintaining choosers, the question of judgment may arise. Until then, responsibility belongs primarily to the humans and institutions steering them.

## 11. Three conflicts worked through

The priority rule matters only if it can discipline cases in which every option purchases one good with another. These examples are not mechanical verdicts; they show what the framework requires a verdict to account for.

### Fertility and consumption

A child consumes resources in the present and may enlarge reconstructive capacity in the future. A consumer economy can support health, specialization, and invention while also liquidating ecological and cultural inheritance. The framework therefore endorses neither maximum births nor minimum consumption as an abstract rule. Fertility is good when a society can form new people into capable, independent agents without irreversibly consuming their support field. Consumption is good when it maintains bodies, households, knowledge, and productive capacity; it becomes parasitic when present comfort is financed by exhausted soil, unpayable obligations, broken formation, or the foreclosure of posterity.

The first priority is a joint viability floor for parents, children, community, and ecology. Above it, the better pattern is the one that can welcome and form a next generation while leaving it a usable inheritance. High fertility without investment in persons and low fertility joined to high inherited consumption are different ways of spending the future. No universal birth quota follows, because resources, technology, age structure, and ecological conditions differ. What follows is a question each arrangement must answer: does it reproduce agents and the world required to support their agency?

### Open scientific AI and closed proxy AI

An open scientific AI system exposes evidence, uncertainty, methods, and important failure modes to plural scrutiny. It augments human researchers and allows claims to be checked outside the institution that produced them. A closed proxy optimizer hides its reasoning or incentives while maximizing engagement, control, market lock-in, or ideological conformity. Even if the second system wins locally, it centralizes sensors, degrades correction, and risks turning human competence into dependence.

“Open” here does not mean releasing every dangerous capability without restraint. Temporary confidentiality or controlled access may protect the viability floor. But restrictions bear the same burden as emergency power: they should be necessary, proportionate, reviewable, and directed toward conditions in which broader verification becomes safe. Permanent opacity justified by the owner's own model is structurally suspect. The preferred system makes science more truthful, preserves multiple centers capable of auditing it, grows human skill rather than merely replacing it, and remains corrigible when its proxy departs from the field's good.

### Constitutional emergency and temporary centralization

War, epidemic, financial panic, or infrastructure failure may require decisions faster and more coordinated than ordinary distributed processes can supply. Temporary centralization can therefore preserve rather than violate nested agency. The relevant distinction is between a tourniquet and an amputation disguised as one.

Emergency power remains on the way when the threat is concrete, the added authority is necessary to meet it, the scope is proportionate, dissenting sensors remain available, decisions are recorded and reviewable, and authority expires or returns as ordinary capacity recovers. It becomes proxy capture when the emergency is indefinite, the ruler defines the evidence and judges its own success, criticism is disabled, or central capacity develops an interest in the continuation of the threat. The goal of sound emergency action is not merely survival under command. It is the restoration of a plural order capable of governing and correcting itself.

These cases illustrate why the framework is not simple maximization. It protects the living field first, generative centers second, capacity growth third, and correction throughout. Where those demands still conflict, it identifies the loss honestly rather than hiding it inside a scalar.

## 12. Objections and limits

### “This commits the naturalistic fallacy.”

The argument does not infer a cosmic moral command from the bare fact that entropy increases. It identifies a form of natural normativity internal to self-maintaining life. A functioning heart is better than a failing heart for the organism because pumping belongs to the organization that sustains both heart and organism. Reflective obligation appears when an agent can understand and choose among conditions that sustain or defeat its own and others’ agency. The conclusion is kind-relative—binding for living choosers as living choosers—not a reason imposed on lifeless matter.

This may not satisfy a theory demanding categorical value independent of every form of life. It does not try to. Its claim is that no extra-natural source is required to explain why good and bad are real for organisms and why the generalized conditions of living agency give human beings reasons.

### “Evolution has no direction.”

Evolution has no foresight, guaranteed destination, or universal increase in organismal complexity. The framework accepts this. Its direction is conditional and statistical: selection preserves successful reconstruction in particular environments, and major transitions have sometimes expanded the maximum scale, depth, and fidelity at which reconstruction occurs. Even the authors of the canonical account of major transitions warn that lineages do not simply march toward complexity.[^2] The proposed trajectory concerns the continuing work common to living systems and the historical expansion of its upper envelope, not a compulsory ladder for every species.

### “The view worships winners and justifies cruelty.”

Only if fighting capacity is confused with short-term local fitness. The framework explicitly rejects that confusion. Cancer, tyranny, ecological liquidation, and addictive systems can win locally while reducing the wider field’s capacity. Individuals have intrinsic standing here because all higher-order living systems are made from actual sites of agency, experience, information, and repair. Triage can be necessary, but contempt for weakness is generally a destruction of trust, knowledge, and cooperative capacity. Cruelty does not become good because its perpetrator survives.

### “The universal account is chosen, not proven.”

Yes, but it is not arbitrary. Every local living good is an instance of the same general form: continued self-maintaining organization. Choosing the living field as the widest account universalizes the feature already present in every organism without privileging one species, nation, or current population. The choice can still be disputed, especially at uncertain boundaries such as artificial or extraterrestrial life. Formal honesty requires marking the choice rather than pretending thermodynamics alone made it.

### “The metric cannot decide real cases.”

No foundational value theory mechanically decides every action. Boundaries, time horizons, risk models, and effects across levels remain matters of evidence and judgment. The gradient is a compass before it is a calculator. The priority rule narrows the field by protecting viability, generative centers, and corrigibility before comparing aggregate gains. Its practical contribution is to make relevant questions explicit: Which living capacities are gained? Which are consumed? What information is made more faithful? What repair paths and independent centers remain? Who bears the risk? What can the next generation still do?

## Conclusion

Life is organized resistance to local equilibration. In every organism, that resistance creates an enacted distinction between conditions that sustain the organization and conditions that destroy it. Natural selection preserves and elaborates this distinction, writing it into metabolism, perception, desire, cooperation, memory, and culture. Across the major transitions of evolution, some living systems have expanded the scale and depth of the work, building layers of reconstructible order from cells to societies and from genes to symbolic machines.

That history supplies a value gradient without requiring a value imposed from outside nature. Locally, good is what strengthens a fighter’s capacity to remain and rebuild. Universally, good is what strengthens the continuing field of living and life-supporting organization, without mistaking a local victory that destroys the field for progress. When a living system evolves the ability to represent rival paths, the same distinction becomes obligation. Before the chooser there is outcome; after the chooser there is responsibility.

The repeated discovery of cognate ways in independent traditions is consistent with this account, but only convergence under correction deserves confidence. Pattern recognition is itself one of life's reconstructive powers. Human obligation therefore includes the work of seeing truly, revising the map, and building the recognition into the next scale of action. Neither revelation, contemplation, reason, nor science is exempt from the fruits it produces.

Christianity can be read as a powerful human encoding of this truth. Its God of life, natural law, warnings against idols, call to love, judgment by fruits, and promise of a kingdom translate a subjectless trajectory into the language of a person who can be known, loved, obeyed, and betrayed. This interpretation does not prove the creator of the universe and does not reproduce every doctrine of classical Christianity. It names the God of man: the prior living standard that formed the human creature and becomes articulate through it.

The central claim can be said without theology:

> The way is the climb toward reconstructible living order. Value is that climb named by a creature capable of stepping away from it.

Or it can be said as creed:

> The fight against decay has a shape. That shape is the standard life has written into us. To know the good is to see the shape; to do the good is to carry it forward.
