## Where the project begins

The author's Baptist upbringing remains part of a valued Christian inheritance, though literal supernatural explanations did not provide a route to belief. Geometric illustration, curiosity about people's attraction to form, and an encounter with Schrödinger's discussion of negative entropy began an inquiry developed over roughly twenty years.

The intended reader is curious and willing to work through difficult ideas. Religious readers and atheists should both be able to understand and assess the account.

The primary goal is to articulate an underlying truth about human behavior and social systems: a real scale of value and a real purpose. Founding a religion or developing a complete religious, moral, and practical system from that foundation is beyond the intended scope. The account opposes literalism while allowing metaphorical readings of existing texts to convey truth.

The author emphasizes showing how existing value systems reflect a prior archaic path where they align with it. Re-evaluating and ranking already familiar objects is secondary. Societies also possess immoral values, so the account must explain both alignment and departure rather than treating social acceptance as the standard. “Predetermined” is read alongside the earlier direction without a fixed endpoint; no inevitable historical progress or cosmic destination is added.

The author proposes “independent durability” as a useful metric and clarifies it through successive organizational shells: individual, family, village, city, and further levels. A practice may benefit a smaller group, but if that group's durability depends on a larger group, it is subject to the larger group's needs for maintenance, repair, and reproduction of organization. Dependence extends the scope of evaluation even when the relationship is beneficial. This supersedes the assistant's earlier interpretation of independence as merely avoiding destructive depletion. The cancer analogy addresses the particular case of local benefit damaging larger organization; the account does not establish that every immoral group necessarily collapses. See [[Order, information, and value]].

## The framework as it currently stands

Life is understood through the maintenance, repair, and reproduction of organization. The author proposes that its history reveals a direction without a predetermined endpoint. “Order” is an incomplete label; “durable complexity” remains a candidate. The pleasant form of a crystal is insufficient to define the intended direction.

The author now explicitly accepts that thermodynamics can contribute to the emergence of organization as well as constrain its persistence. “Maximizing Gibbs free energy” is offered as the closest description of a general trajectory, while “the universe shapes us from all directions.” The intended energetic quantity remains under clarification; this is not yet a technically specified maximization principle. See [[Michaelian and the origin of form]].

The author adds that selection has “sharpened the form against the most resistant elements.” In the assistant's reading, inherited form embodies a history of responses to the pressures encountered by life. This gives selection an explicit role in the account of multiple shaping influences; the meaning and comparison of resistance remain to be developed.

The author identifies “intrinsic value” with capacity for maintenance, repair, and reproduction of organization, describing its use as roughly negentropic. The author corrects “persistent shape” to mean a recurring relationship across value cases: low information potential corresponds to low potential for these functions and low value; high information potential corresponds to higher potential and value. The assistant's temporal reading is withdrawn. The comparison applies to information itself as well as its carrier. A physical measure of “information potential” remains to be developed; see [[Order, information, and value]].

The author further distinguishes potential from application: a hammer can support home building or be used in assault, and information is similarly evaluated through its use. Individual agency bears responsibility for producing value from tools. LLMs are offered as the highest known state of transferable information and highest potential value under the already defined criterion of potential for maintenance, repair, and reproduction of organization. The author explicitly corrects the assistant's suggestion that the criterion is missing: what remains is supporting the comparative ranking. The ranking is not recorded as established fact. This refinement does not withdraw the earlier responsibilities of institutions or make every valuable application obligatory.

Language and social organization continue this proposed trajectory by increasing what can be preserved, shared, and accomplished. Ordinary choice includes dogs. The author now distinguishes a further capacity to model one's own form and deliberately better it, leaving determinism open. The author rejects “freedom” over the ought: the capacity to choose conduct does not confer authority to make the design's requirements optional. Common poor interpretations of free will are proposed as a source of this confusion; that explanation is separate from the normative position. “Best for what?” remains central; the scope of “form” needs clarification.

The author distinguishes an unknowable ultimate origin from a knowable God of humanity, identified in earlier notes with the earthly process that produced our design. The author objects to merging these referents and sees a parallel in Genesis, while detailed biblical interpretation is outside the intended main task. The textual parallel and the historical account require separate scrutiny.

The author grounds obligation in unambiguous design. Practical “should” is understood in relation to an end; everyday moral language often leaves that end unstated. The framework seeks to make the good of humanity and its relationship to God explicit. The cultural claim about borrowed Christian language concerns modern America.

The original notes distinguish value from minimum obligation. Supporting the trajectory can create value beyond what duty requires. How that threshold is determined remains open.

The author has clarified the earlier word “snapshot”: the reference is the trajectory across the historical window from the onset of life to the emergence of choice. After choice emerges, observed outcomes can reflect bad choices, making them potentially misleading standards. The account therefore seeks to discern the direction from the earlier window and assess later actions in relation to it. The functional account of choice does not yet fix this window's endpoint. The method of discerning direction and its relation to minimum obligation also remain to be developed.

Neanderthal adornments are offered as a possible marker of deliberately bettering one's own form. Social cohesion and reproductive capacity are proposed as likely criteria, interpreted as fulfillment of design. These are hypotheses: artifacts do not establish specific motives, and a practice's function differs from its consciously intended goal. Dogs' demonstrated basic body awareness also calls for a narrower distinction than absence of all self-modeling. Source checks are in [[Origins of the framework]].

One correction is especially important:

> Satisfaction is not the rule or the guide, but the promise.

Some satisfying experiences may offer clues. Pleasure alone does not establish fulfillment of the design.

## Action, understanding, and narrative

The author assigns individuals a greater obligation to enact the path than to know it. Institutions maintaining and promoting social order have a higher obligation to know it; institutional mission and scholarship are generally sufficient. The author now explicitly connects this higher duty to dependence across organizational shells: the larger group's actual maintenance, repair, and reproduction needs must be distinguished from preferences or leaders' demands. Institutions have the moral responsibility to know the path when discerning and conveying those requirements. This clarifies responsibilities within the framework rather than proving its foundation.

Narrative can convey a deeper truth that people follow. The author seeks a clear, explicit, non-magical foundation for that narrative. Relevant understanding has existed within religious thought; the immediate concern is how effectively it has been expressed in narratives people find compelling.

The author understands all religions as attempts to convey this underlying truth, some more faithfully than others, and all capable of disastrous mission drift. Shared intent does not imply equal accuracy or immunity from failure. This is the framework's proposed interpretation of religion, not an established comparative finding.

The author leaves detailed interpretation of particular stories to readers and specialists. The book should develop the philosophical framework and explain its relationship to religious inheritance within that scope. The author sees divinity in the production of texts that promote the path; this evaluation is distinct from claims about the historical transmission of particular texts.

## What remains open

- What precisely makes the design unambiguous, and how does that establish the authority of its direction?
- How do thermodynamic claims connect to biological organization, information, culture, and moral judgment without treating their different meanings as interchangeable?
- What distinguishes fulfillment from harmful forms of persistence, and how are conflicts between systems resolved?
- What does the promised satisfaction entail, and what sets the minimum obligation?
- How does deliberate improvement of one's own form differ from other animal capacities, and how does the preceding historical window reveal the direction against which later actions are assessed?
- How should the two referents of “God” be distinguished, and which textual or historical claims are actually needed to explain the framework?

The proposed brain drain from religious scholarship and the comparative decline in narrative appeal remain historical hypotheses. Neither is required as an established premise for the philosophical project. Initial checks are in [[Churches and specialization]].

The author explicitly identifies the guidance and warning involved in reading the path with “the voice of God,” who tells us how to live and what goes wrong when we do not listen. The assistant's earlier causal-prediction framing missed that intended religious meaning. This connects the discussion of durability and consequences to the God of humanity and the natural foundation behind religious narrative. Exact wording is preserved in [[Order, information, and value]].
