## Reference points from the author's account

These are research tasks prompted by [[Origins of the framework]], with initial checks identified where available.

- **Schrödinger and negentropy:** identify the passage or presentation that influenced the author; examine the scientific meaning and the author's extrapolation separately.
- **Michaelian and pigment-world abiogenesis:** examine the proposed continuity between photon-driven molecular formation and later living organization. The author asks whether genesis itself is “within form” and clarifies “ex uno, omnia” as referring to the physical process underlying life. Source details and remaining distinctions are in [[Michaelian and the origin of form]].
- **Gibbs free energy and the proposed trajectory:** the author's phrase “maximizing Gibbs free energy” requires clarification of the system, quantity, constraints, and timescale. The expanded Michaelian note records checks against IUPAC definitions, a primary MIT lecture, and Branscomb's research report on coupled processes. Distinguish available energy, useful power, and dissipation; do not silently substitute one for another or represent a maximization principle as established.
- **Aesthetic attraction to order:** clarify the claim of near universality and find evidence relevant to the particular examples, including geometric preferences.
- **Adornments and the emergence of choice:** the author proposes Neanderthal adornment as a possible marker. [Radovčić et al. (2015), Krapina eagle talons](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0119802), was consulted for the artifact evidence and its interpretation as jewelry and symbolic activity. Keep that inference distinct from first emergence of choice and from the framework's moral threshold. See [[Origins of the framework]].
- **Dogs and modeling oneself:** the abstract of [Lenkei et al. (2021)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33602955/) reports evidence of body awareness in an obstacle task; full paper access was blocked. The author distinguishes ordinary choice from modeling and bettering one's own form. Separate bodily self-representation from imagined improvement, and test the proposed comparative boundary. The source does not settle the latter capacity.
- **Free will and freedom of the ought:** [Catholic Catechism §§1731–1735](https://www.vatican.va/content/catechism/en/part_three/section_one/chapter_one/article_3/i_freedom_and_responsibility.html), consulted directly, connects freedom with reason, formation of one's life, and goodness. The author subsequently clarified that the central position rejects freedom over the ought, with common poor interpretations of free will proposed separately as a source of confusion. This text is comparative background, not a rebuttal to that position; it does not establish the proposed history of popular understanding or represent all Christian traditions.
- **Language and organization:** define compression, enrichment, and shareable information; investigate how the proposed continuity with living organization would be supported.
- **Order, information, and value:** see [[Order, information, and value]] for the author's stone–diamond–book comparison. Shannon's primary introduction distinguishes communication measures from meaning; selected passages of Kolchinsky and Wolpert (2018) supply a framework relating information to viability through interventions. Neither provides a universal moral scale. The author specifies a recurring relationship between information potential, potential for maintenance/repair/reproduction of organization, and value. “Persistent shape” means recurrence across cases; the earlier temporal reading is withdrawn. Specify the intended information potential before assigning a physical measure.
- **Tools, LLMs, and application:** the author distinguishes potential from value produced through application and assigns responsibility to individual agency. The primary abstract of [Brown et al. (2020)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14165) was consulted for a historical example of broad language-model applicability. It does not establish the author's superlative ranking. The author emphasizes that the criterion is already defined: potential for maintenance, repair, and reproduction of organization. Research should investigate the comparative claim under that criterion, not treat the absence of a physical unit as the absence of a value definition. See [[Order, information, and value]].
- **Christianity, Greek philosophy, and Western development:** turn the broad account of inheritance into particular propositions, practices, texts, and periods that can be compared.
- **Literalism, modernism, and postmodernism:** establish which movements and historical divergence the author means before assigning a chronology or causal account. Specify the two proposed paths of deterioration.
- **Process philosophy:** the author confirms Whitehead or process theology. Selected passages of *Process and Reality* and John Cobb's exposition were consulted for [[Philosophical comparisons]]. Identify what the author accepts and considers incomplete or insufficiently incorporated; do not assume Whitehead's account lacks value or purpose.
- **Genesis terminology and textual transmission:** [Genesis 1:1](https://biblehub.com/text/genesis/1-1.htm) has Elohim and [Genesis 2:4](https://biblehub.com/text/genesis/2-4.htm) has YHWH Elohim. Compare the author's philosophical distinction with [Konrad Schmid's account of compilation](https://www.thetorah.com/article/genesis-two-creation-accounts-compiled-and-interpreted-as-one) and scholarship on [Mesopotamian flood traditions](https://www.thetorah.com/article/the-mesopotamian-origin-of-the-biblical-flood-story). Keep textual observations, historical reconstructions, and the framework's interpretation separate. Detailed biblical extrapolation is outside the author's intended main task.
- **Churches, specialization, and institutional knowledge:** see [[Churches and specialization]] for the author's proposed brain-drain explanation, initial chronological checks, cases of theological engagement with evolution and process thought, and the distinction between scholarly production and influence on ordinary teaching.
- **“Written in our hearts”:** the author's phrase has relevant textual parallels in [Romans 2:14–15](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Romans+2%3A14-15&version=NKJV) and [Jeremiah 31:33](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Jeremiah+31%3A33&version=NKJV). Wording located in NKJV; the intended passage is unconfirmed. Examine the different contexts before comparing the author's interpretation with them. These passages do not by themselves establish the framework's claim about promised satisfaction.
