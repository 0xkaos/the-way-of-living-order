### Consequences and ways to examine them

Two proposed failure patterns should remain distinct:

- A practice erodes the group's own capacity to maintain, repair, or reproduce its organization.
- A practice benefits a local group while eroding those capacities in a larger supporting organization. Looking only at the local group's persistence would conceal the damage.

Assistant proposal for applying the metric: identify the practice, affected systems, and relevant timescale; examine what capacities are renewed or depleted and what outside support compensates for the losses. A possible thought experiment is an institution retaining its position while consuming inherited trust or expertise faster than these are renewed. Its continuing existence would not by itself establish that its practices maintain those conditions.

The author's word “doomed” states a strong predictive claim. A narrower assistant formulation is that persistent depletion creates a maintenance deficit that must be repaired, compensated for, or eventually borne as diminished capacity. The framework has not yet demonstrated that every immoral practice necessarily produces its own group's collapse, or that every collapse diagnoses immorality. Durability is proposed as evidence under the existing value criterion, not as a replacement equating all longevity with goodness. The earlier question about conflicts between several viable systems remains open.

This connects with the cancer example in the original `Life is matter organized.txt` and the older discussion of “nesting” collected in [[Older paper - ideas to revisit]]. It does not automatically endorse every formulation in those earlier drafts. Unlike the corrected “persistent shape,” which means recurrence across value cases, this new candidate metric explicitly concerns continued functioning over time.

## Four different properties

| Property | What it concerns | What it does not establish by itself |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Physical organization and material properties | The arrangement, stability, and behavior of the material. | A universal ranking of usefulness or moral value. |
| Information-storage capacity | Distinguishable, sufficiently stable states that can be written and read using a specified system. | That any valuable message has actually been stored. |
| Meaning or content | What an inscription expresses within a language or other interpretive system. | That the content is true, useful, or beneficial. |
| Functional contribution | What access to the object or information enables a living system to maintain, repair, reproduce, learn, or otherwise do. | That every benefit to one system is good overall, or that a complete common scale has been established. |

These are distinctions for analysis, not four mandatory evolutionary stages. A stone can provide material support without functioning as a symbolic message. A message can be meaningful and harmful. Fiction can contribute through understanding or formation without being a factual instruction manual.

Shannon's communication theory distinguishes messages and measures their statistical uncertainty and transmission requirements while setting their meaning aside for that engineering problem. Its information measure is therefore not a direct measure of truth or benefit. A source of unpredictable symbols can have high Shannon entropy without supplying useful knowledge. [Shannon, *A Mathematical Theory of Communication* (1948)](https://people.math.harvard.edu/~ctm/home/text/others/shannon/entropy/entropy.pdf), introduction and communication-system description consulted.

## Applying the distinctions to the three objects

**Ordinary stone.** Its potential contribution depends on its properties and circumstances: for example, it may supply material support or serve as a tool. It can also carry an inscription or provide evidence to an investigator. Its being a stone does not exclude informational uses.

**Diamond.** Its characteristic structure supplies particular material possibilities; the author's aesthetic and mechanical examples concern how those properties can matter to living beings. Crystalline regularity does not by itself provide a numerical increment of value above an unspecified stone. The stone, task, quantities compared, and available alternatives matter. “Diamond is slightly more valuable” is the author's proposed illustration, not a result derived from thermodynamics.

**Book.** Its distinctive contribution in this comparison is the accessible arrangement of its marks and the possibilities that arrangement enables through readers, language, and action. The ordinary book is designed to preserve and communicate text; that does not demonstrate a larger fundamental physical information capacity than every stone or diamond. A stone inscribed with the same usable knowledge could share the informational contribution.

The relevant system for a book's informational effect extends beyond paper and ink: book, actual or realistically possible readers, interpretive competence, and the material conditions for action. This preserves potential value without assuming an arbitrary imaginary decoder could make any pattern equally useful.

## A controlled thought experiment

Compare a usable manual for repairing a water pump with a version made by scrambling its words. Keep the amount of paper and ink and the inventory of printed words the same. This does not keep every physical or statistical property identical; the meaningful arrangement is precisely what has changed.

The copies may have very similar bulk material and energetic properties. Yet people who understand the usable manual may be able to repair a pump that they could not repair with the scrambled version. The difference concerns what the pattern enables in the larger system. It cannot be inferred simply from how much paper, ink, or chemical energy is present.

The book contributes by guiding the use of energy and materials supplied through readers and their environment. To estimate that contribution, one could compare repair success, resources expended, reliability, or the ability to teach the procedure, under specified conditions. These are possible measurements of consequences, not an already validated universal unit of value.

A competing example should remain possible: a useful stone can contribute more than a misleading book. The framework should explain such reversals rather than rank object categories unconditionally.

## A particularly relevant physics connection

Kolchinsky and Wolpert propose a definition of semantic information based on information about the environment that contributes causally to a system's continued existence. They examine interventions that remove correlations and compare the resulting viability. Their framework requires a chosen system, dynamics, timescale, and viability measure; the paper discusses limitations of its chosen measure. [*Semantic information, autonomous agency and non-equilibrium statistical physics* (2018)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.08053), abstract and selected sections on viability and interventions consulted in the [author-hosted paper](https://artemyk.github.io/assets/pdf/papers/Kolchinsky_Wolpert_2018_Semantic%20information,%20autonomous%20agency%20and%20non-equilibrium%20statistical%20physics.pdf).

This is a close research connection for the book's proposed account of informational value. It supplies a way to ask what difference information makes to a specified system, not a proof of the author's entire moral framework or a ready-made ranking of books. The scrambled-manual example above is an explanatory analogy, not a claim that we have implemented their mathematical intervention.

## Consequence for the energy discussion

This example adds a dimension to the tentative emphasis on access to usable energy in [[Michaelian and the origin of form]]: the organization that enables that energy and other resources to be used effectively. More energy available and better information about using it are distinguishable contributions. Neither an object's Gibbs energy nor a count of bits alone supplies the ranking the author is seeking.

The proposed normative standard gives maintenance, repair, and reproduction their evaluative importance. Physics and causal analysis can help investigate what supports those processes; the distinction between describing a contribution and establishing its moral authority remains visible.

## Application, tools, and individual agency

Author's further statement:

> Regarding contents of the book, like with any tool, it's value lies in it's application. A hammer is a great value for the sake of home building, not so great if it's being used for an assault. LLM's another interesting example, the highest known state of transferrable information, the highest potential value to maintenance, repair, and reproduction of organization, but only if so used, the responsibility then falling on individual agency to produce value from tools.

The author distinguishes what a tool makes possible from what its application contributes. A hammer's application in building a home supports the proposed evaluative direction; its use in assault can act against it. The contents of a book are likewise assessed through application. Information potential does not guarantee a beneficial outcome.

Assistant-proposed distinctions:

| Question | What is being evaluated |
| --- | --- |
| What can the tool enable? | Potential contribution and the range of possible applications. |
| How effectively does it achieve a chosen result? | Instrumental effectiveness relative to that result. |
| What does this application contribute to maintenance, repair, and reproduction of organization? | Realized value under the author's proposed standard. |
| How does an agent direct the tool? | The exercise of choice and responsibility for its application. |

The hammer example shows why effectiveness at an agent's chosen task cannot by itself determine value. This connects to the author's rejection of freedom over the ought: agents choose applications, while the proposed standard supplies their evaluation. The standard does not become whatever purpose a user happens to select.

This distinction between potential and application is now directly prompted by the author. It does **not** restore the withdrawn interpretation of “persistent shape” as durability between uses; that phrase continues to mean a recurring pattern across value cases.

The author places responsibility on individual agency to produce value from tools. Do not enlarge this into a claim that all responsibility belongs exclusively to the final user, that the design of every tool is morally neutral, or that every possible increase in value is obligatory. The previously stated higher epistemic responsibilities of institutions and distinction between value and minimum obligation remain in place. How those responsibilities apply to particular tools remains to be developed.
