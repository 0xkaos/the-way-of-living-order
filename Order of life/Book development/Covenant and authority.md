# Covenant and authority

## Author's statement

> I think it's largely a covenant offered by the path, you must do x to be in accordance with the tragectory and accordance with the trajectory will be rewarding. In my mind "god" "the path" and "the trajectory" are equivalent and interchangeable.

## What this changes

The authority of the ought is covenantal rather than derivational. The framework does not cross the is–ought gap by producing a categorical "must" from natural facts; it describes an offer whose terms, promise, and consequences exist prior to and independent of any agent's response. "Must do x" is a conditional of accordance (what the terms require), not a free-floating command.

Assistant reading to confirm: on this account there is **no further "must" behind the covenant**. The demand for a goal-independent categorical obligation is treated as a confusion, consistent with the author's earlier claim that every practical "should" implies an end (C16).

## What it unifies

- **C14 (satisfaction as promise):** a covenant is terms plus promise. "Satisfaction is not the rule or the guide, but the promise" supplies the promissory half.
- **C15 (unambiguous design):** unambiguity is the determinacy of the terms. Rejecting "freedom over the ought" means compliance is chosen while terms and consequences are not; choice operates inside the covenant, never over it.
- **C16 (should implies an end):** the covenant framing accepts ends-relative obligation consistently. The end is given by the design, not selected by the agent.
- **C38 (voice of God):** guidance and warning are the covenant's sanction structure.
- **C5 (ought appears with choice):** pre-choice life keeps the covenant without being bound; only a being capable of knowing breach is under obligation. The emergence of choice is where the offer is first received, not where the trajectory is defined.
- **C40–C41 (soul, afterlife, damnation):** the ledger of the covenant is settled in enduring good, not only in felt satisfaction. This answers the successful-cheat objection and makes the afterlife account load-bearing rather than peripheral.
- **C7 (two referents):** biblical covenants are made with YHWH, the knowable rule-giving referent, never with the pre-creation ground. The unknowable origin is not a party to the covenant; the author's Genesis 2:4 distinction maps onto the covenant structure.
- **Anscombe positioning:** modern moral philosophy kept lawlike "ought" after abandoning the lawgiver. The framework's answer is neither dropping the law nor re-grounding it in reason, but restoring the lawgiver, naturalized, and restoring the law's native covenantal form: terms, promise, sanction.

## The identity claim

"God," "the path," and "the trajectory" are equivalent and interchangeable (author-stated). This tightens C7: the knowable God of humanity is strictly identical with the trajectory, not merely associated with it. "Covenant offered by the path" and "covenant offered by God" are the same sentence in the framework.

## The statistical promise

The covenant's promise is a strong tendency, not a guarantee (C44). Implications:

- **Absence is not a verdict.** Erased good is a real and permanent loss that "proves nothing" against the person or the path. This resolves the moral-luck question in [[Soul and afterlife]]: damnation-as-absence can occur without breach. C41 should be read accordingly.
- **Symmetry to preserve.** The same statistics cut the other way: a breach that luckily leaves enduring good proves nothing *for* the breacher. Conduct is therefore assessed by its tendency under the terms, while the ledger records what in fact remains. Two different registers — the keeping of the covenant and the surviving of the good — and judgment, where it applies at all, attaches to the first. This re-aligns the covenant with the conduct-based duty structure (C5, C6, C19).
- **"Doomed" softens consistently.** C37's strong predictive wording becomes a claim of statistical tendency: departures from the path are disfavored, not guaranteed to collapse. This closes an open caution in that claim's record.
- **Reproduction is the design's hedge — assistant proposal.** The author's durability aside ("if it were more durable the information would have lived") points at something stronger than blame: the terms themselves already answer tail risk. Reproduction of organization — the third term of the criterion — is the covenant's own response to the statistical nature of loss: copies, redundancy, transmission. The Library of Alexandria illustrates a single-copy failure of the reproduction term as much as bad luck. Guard: no finite redundancy defeats every tail event, so this must never become outcome-blame against the erased. The author notes a non-manmade tragedy may illustrate the point better.

## Must, should, ought — author query, October 16, 2026

From the author's markup of [[One-page statement]]:

> in the context of a covenant, must is essentially just a sharper version of should, but the promise is the same. The promise is if you act in accordance with the path/your design it will likely be good and pleasant, if you don't it will likely be bad and unpleasant, so you must/should/ought to act in accordance or bad things will probably happen. ... Not sure how much stastical threshholds need to be addressed or not to distinguish must from should or ought.

Assistant analysis, awaiting the author's confirmation:

- **Confirmed in kind:** once categorical obligation is rejected (C16), must, should, and ought are all ends-relative; none carries a different metaphysics. The same promise and sanction stand behind all three.
- **Refinement proposed:** rather than a single intensity dial, map the words onto the framework's existing two-tier structure. **"Must" marks the minimum threshold (C6):** below it, conduct is breach of covenant. **"Should" ranges over the value gradient above the threshold:** counsel toward greater accordance, good but not obligatory. This gives "must" a non-arbitrary referent — the duty floor — instead of mere emphasis, and it matches ordinary usage (must = required, should = recommended).
- **No statistical thresholds needed for the distinction.** The statistics belong to the *consequences* (the promise and sanction are tendencies, C44), not to the demand. Two orthogonal axes: how much is demanded (threshold vs. gradient) and how reliably consequences follow (statistical). Quantifying the second would not sharpen the first.
- **Guard:** "likely be good and pleasant" should not become the book's wording for the promise — pleasant drifts toward the hedonist reading the heroin example blocks. Keep "rewarding/satisfying" as promise, never as measure.
