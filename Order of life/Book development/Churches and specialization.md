# Churches and specialization

Research note opened October 15, 2026. This is a first check of a proposed explanation, not a completed history. The author's original wording is preserved in [[Origins of the framework]].

## Author's proposal

Institutional mission and scholarship generally satisfy the higher responsibility to know the path. The author suspects that modern churches have lost sight of a natural foundation, partly because specialization and new intellectual careers drew talent away from religious scholarship and weakened their ability to engage with evolution and process philosophy.

The individual/institutional distinction is a refinement of the framework. The historical hypothesis is a possible application; it is not a premise required to establish the whole framework.

## Latest clarification

The author subsequently stated that relevant understanding has existed, but has failed to frame a sufficiently compelling narrative for many people compared with earlier times. The author accepts narrative expressions of deeper truth and seeks a clear, explicit, non-magical foundation for them.

The current emphasis is therefore on the relationship between scholarship, narrative, and its reception. The brain-drain hypothesis remains unverified and has not been explicitly withdrawn. It should not dominate the account or be treated as its established cause. A comparative decline in narrative appeal is also a historical claim requiring evidence and defined scope.

## Separate the proposed explanations

- **Loss of intellectual talent:** fewer or less capable scholars entered or remained in religious study. Needs evidence about recruitment, careers, training, and intellectual output; specialization alone does not establish it.
- **Separation of disciplines:** knowledge became distributed across fields with limited exchange. Needs particular institutional histories and evidence about communication between fields.
- **Failure to bring scholarship into teaching:** relevant work existed but did not sufficiently influence ministerial education, preaching, or ordinary practice. Needs comparisons between scholarship, curricula, sermons, and congregational materials.
- **Doctrinal or institutional resistance:** ideas may have been understood and rejected for reasons other than lack of scholarly capacity. Investigate those reasons before inferring inability.

These are possible explanations to distinguish, not findings that have been demonstrated. Several could operate together, differently across traditions and periods.

## Define the scope before expanding the research

Choose a tradition, period, and observable example of the alleged loss of natural foundation. The author's earlier focus on modern America and Baptist upbringing may provide a starting case, but does not establish the intended scope of this broader claim.

Clarify whether “liturgical study” means the study of worship specifically or theological and clerical scholarship more broadly. Preserve the original wording until the scope is settled.
