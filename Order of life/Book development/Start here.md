# Developing the book

This is a working space for turning the existing notes into an argument and then a manuscript. The organization, interpretations, and outline here are assistant proposals, pending your corrections. Statements attributed to your notes are starting positions, not findings that have been independently established.

Purpose clarified in conversation: articulate an underlying truth about human behavior and social systems, including a real scale of value and a real purpose. The author understands religions as attempts to convey that truth, with unequal proximity and the possibility of disastrous mission drift. The account opposes literalism while allowing metaphorical understandings of existing texts.

The author further clarifies that the main aim is to show how existing value systems reflect a prior, archaic path where they are aligned with it, while recognizing that societies also hold immoral values. Evaluating and ranking familiar objects anew is secondary. Examples should reveal the proposed relationship between existing valuations and the underlying direction; social endorsement does not itself establish alignment.

The book develops the philosophical foundation. Founding a new religion or elaborating a complete religious, moral, and practical system from it is outside scope. Detailed extrapolation into particular religious stories and practices is left to readers and specialists.

## A repeatable session

1. Choose one question or example.
2. Record your answer in your own words.
3. Have the assistant restate it and identify any added assumptions. Correct the restatement.
4. Develop the strongest objection and decide what survives it.
5. Draft a short passage, if the idea is ready for prose.
6. Save changes in the relevant note and record any decisions in the notebook.

A session can end with a better question. Every session does not need to produce manuscript pages.

## First milestones

- A one-page statement you recognize as your position, with important uncertainties visible.
- An argument map with stable definitions and the principal objections.
- A short sample essay that tests both the argument and the voice.
- A chapter plan revised around what the sample teaches us.
- A complete rough manuscript, followed by separate argument, structure, and prose revisions.

## Working arrangement

You supply the commitments, experiences, judgments, and final choices. The assistant can interview, organize, research, challenge, draft, and edit. Proposed additions should be labeled so that fluent wording does not quietly become a new philosophical commitment.

Use the existing Obsidian notes for now. Save durable conclusions here rather than leaving them only in chat. Before substantial rewrites, keep a dated copy of the passage or chapter; record changes in meaning separately from wording changes. Citation software and publishing tools can be chosen when the source collection and manuscript need them.
