## What Michaelian proposes

Karo Michaelian's pigment-world account places ultraviolet light at the center of abiogenesis. He proposes that simple precursors formed organic molecules that absorbed this light and rapidly dissipated its energy as heat into surrounding water. “Pigment” here includes molecules absorbing ultraviolet radiation; it need not mean a visibly colored substance.

In this account, photon-driven formation and proliferation of these molecules preceded their familiar biological roles. The optical behavior of fundamental biomolecules is offered as evidence for that history. The proposed continuity runs from early molecular dissipation to later biological organization and wider use of the solar spectrum. These are Michaelian's explanatory claims, not conclusions independently established by this note. [“The Pigment World: Life's Origins as Photon-Dissipating Pigments” (2024)](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-1729/14/7/912), abstract, introduction, and selected conclusion passages consulted through the Europe PMC full-text copy.

His earlier presentation also proposes UV light and daily temperature cycling as a route to nucleic-acid replication without enzymes. This is a proposed mechanism, not a demonstrated complete transition from simple chemistry to an autonomous organism. [“Thermodynamic dissipation theory for the origin of life” (2011)](https://esd.copernicus.org/articles/2/37/2011/), abstract consulted.

## The thermodynamic distinction to retain

The later supplied passage and more detailed source checks are in [[Dissipative structures and living organization]]. They distinguish the established thermodynamic background from Michaelian's particular origin hypothesis and from a universal maximum-dissipation principle.

The earlier language of life opposing disorder needs a distinction between the living system and its surroundings. Local organization can be maintained while the total process produces entropy. Incoming energy can sustain a system far from equilibrium while energy is dissipated into its environment. Michaelian explicitly places life's proposed origin and persistence within this dissipative account. [Michaelian (2011)](https://esd.copernicus.org/articles/2/37/2011/).

Consequently, “resisting decay” can describe a living system maintaining its form without implying that life violates or reverses the second law. Dissipation is also part of the process that makes such maintenance possible. In Michaelian's hypothesis, it contributes to explaining the emergence of the relevant molecular organization itself.

Do not silently equate three different claims:

1. A living system maintains organization through exchanges with its environment.
2. Particular forms originated because their formation and proliferation enhanced photon dissipation.
3. Greater dissipation supplies the moral standard for human conduct.

The second is the specific historical and mechanistic proposal under consideration. The third is not established by either of the first two, and the author has not endorsed it. Likewise, the second law alone does not identify one inevitable organism, historical path, or moral outcome.

### Technical distinctions for the wording

Gibbs energy is a specific thermodynamic quantity, conventionally written G = H − TS. In the familiar closed-system setting at fixed temperature and pressure, without imposed non-expansion work, spontaneous change decreases G toward equilibrium. Stable equilibrium is associated with a minimum, not a maximum. Sources: [IUPAC's Gibbs-energy definition](https://goldbook.iupac.org/terms/view/G02629) and [equilibrium entry](https://goldbook.iupac.org/terms/view/14656), indexed definitions consulted because direct access was blocked; [Gian Paolo Beretta's MIT thermodynamics lecture](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-43-advanced-thermodynamics-spring-2024/1Q7kBt43LW4oPrh3pxYjs2xxe7HWsC1oR_transcript.pdf), selected passages concerning availability and equilibrium minima consulted.

That closed-system criterion does not imply that a living system cannot build energy stores or maintain chemical gradients. Living systems use coupled processes: an energetically downhill process can drive an uphill one. [Elbert Branscomb's “Thermodynamics of Life” research report](https://astrobiology.nasa.gov/nai/annual-reports/2013/uiuc/thermodynamics-of-life/index.html), project summary and discussion of coupling consulted. This source supports the coupling distinction; it does not establish a universal biological maximization principle.

For the author's intended trajectory, distinguish:

- **Available capacity:** usable free-energy differences that the system can access or maintain, relative to its surroundings.
- **Useful power:** the rate at which energy is converted into work that supports the system's activities.
- **Dissipation:** irreversible degradation of available work potential, associated with entropy production.

These quantities are related but not interchangeable. “Maximizing Gibbs free energy” should not be silently corrected into “maximizing dissipation,” nor accepted as a settled physical law. A stock of available energy, a rate of useful work, and a rate of dissipation can suggest different trajectories.
