## Definitions to settle

- **Organization / order:** The author now offers “durable complexity” as a candidate and explicitly distinguishes the intended direction from the pleasant form of a crystal. What must be present besides persistence? Does the term change meaning between a cell and an institution?
- **Entropy:** Is a particular use a measurable physical quantity, an analogy, or a term for functional breakdown? Specify which before using it in an argument.
- **Design:** What makes a trait part of the design to fulfill, rather than something inherited that ought to be resisted?
- **Ambiguity:** The author rejects ambiguity in explaining obligation but now identifies ambiguity in using outcomes after choice emerges as standards, since they can reflect bad choices. A distinction between the direction itself and the interpretation of later outcomes is an assistant proposal awaiting confirmation.
- **Trajectory:** The author explicitly proposes direction without a predetermined endpoint. How is that direction identified, and what relationship does it have to authority over future choices?
- **Value:** Value for a particular system, moral value, or a proposed common measure?
- **Independent durability / shells:** The author clarifies that dependence on a larger group means the smaller group is not independently durable relative to it and is subject to its maintenance, repair, and reproduction needs. Local benefit cannot settle the assessment where durability depends on larger organization. This supersedes the assistant's definition through absence of destructive depletion; see C37 and C39. The relevant degrees and kinds of dependence remain to be developed.
- **Intrinsic value / informational value:** Capacity for maintenance, repair, and reproduction of organization, roughly negentropic when utilized. The author corrects “persistent shape” to mean a recurring relationship across value cases, not persistence over time: low/high information potential corresponds to low/high potential for these functions and value. Keep physical storage capacity, meaning, and functional potential distinguishable; see C33 and [[Order, information, and value]].
- **Ought / denial:** What conduct falls below the threshold? Are mistakes, incapacity, and deliberate refusal treated differently?
- **Should / end:** Practical “should” presupposes a goal in the author's account. The author identifies unambiguous design as the source of authority for the shared end. “Good for group cohesion” illustrates an explicit end; it is not yet a definition of all human good.
- **Enact / know:** The author assigns greater emphasis to enactment for individuals and a higher duty of knowledge to institutions maintaining social order. Institutional mission and scholarship are generally sufficient in the author's clarification; assess particular failures through specific institutional examples.
- **God:** The author distinguishes an unknowable ultimate origin from the knowable source of human obligation. Clarify the kind of distinction and the sense of change and permanence involved; avoid assuming that the Genesis terminology establishes it historically.
- **Choice / freedom:** Ordinary choice includes dogs. The author locates the relevant further capacity in modeling and deliberately bettering one's own form, leaving determinism open. “Best for what?” remains central. The assistant's proposed definition through imagining an improved self awaits confirmation; do not equate ordinary choice, bodily awareness, and deliberate formation of oneself.
- **Form:** Appearance is the immediate adornment example. Whether the author's term also includes abilities, habits, character, or collective organization remains to be clarified.
- **Freedom of the ought / arbitrariness:** The author rejects freedom over the ought: choosing conduct does not confer authority to make the design's requirements optional. The author separately proposes that common poor interpretations of free will produce this confusion. Do not reframe the position primarily as a critique of Christian doctrine. Determinism, the range of permissible actions, and minimum obligation remain distinct questions.
- **Historical window / emergence of choice:** The author clarified “snapshot” as the window from life's onset to the emergence of the relevant freedom, across which the trajectory can be discerned. Neanderthal adornment is a proposed marker of modeling and bettering one's own form. The precise historical boundary and method of inferring direction remain open.
- **Narrative / foundation:** The author accepts narrative expressions of deeper truth and seeks an explicit, non-magical foundation. Specify how a narrative expresses that foundation and how its accuracy can be assessed.
- **Purpose / religious scope:** The author seeks a real value scale and purpose underlying human behavior and social systems. This continues the account of direction without a predetermined endpoint. All religions are understood as attempts at conveying that truth; the book does not undertake a new religion or a comprehensive practical code.
- **Mission drift:** Any religion can depart disastrously from the truth it exists to convey. Clarify how such departure is recognized within the framework, including in favored traditions. The specific older account of proxy capture remains available for commentary rather than automatically adopted in full.
- **Satisfaction / promise:** The author places satisfaction as a consequence of fulfilling the design and allows some pleasurable experiences to offer clues. It is not the criterion of fulfillment. Specify what “promise” commits the account to.

## Differences between the existing versions

1. **Bracketing and unknowability.** One version declines to address ultimate origins; another says those origins are unknowable. The latter adds a claim requiring its own argument.
2. **Capacity and accumulated complexity.** Supporting viable organization may differ from maximizing ordered, information-rich persistence. Decide whether these formulations are interchangeable.
3. **Finite duty and open-ended fulfillment.** The threshold account needs to remain visible wherever the broader demand to continue the trajectory appears.
4. **Origins and necessity.** A constrained historical outcome need not be the only possible outcome. Specify the intended force of “non-arbitrary.”
5. **Ends and universal obligation.** Practical “should” is relative to an end; an earlier thesis says obligation applies whether acknowledged or not. The author now locates the end's authority in unambiguous design. The intended connection is clarified; its justification remains to be developed.

- How does the account understand care for people who cannot maintain themselves or reproduce?
- Can dismantling a durable institution be good? Can accepting an ending be good?
- What would count against the framework, rather than being redescribed as denial or disorder?
